Note: $integral.double_D 1 d A$ = area of A.

> **Polar form**
> The **polar form** of the double integral $integral.double_D f(x, y) d A$ is:
> $$ integral_alpha^beta integral_g(theta)^h(theta) f(r cos theta, r sin theta) r d r d theta $$

![[15.3 Double Integrals in Polar Coordinates 2025-11-10 12.44.42.excalidraw]]

To convert an integral to polar form:
1. Rewrite bounds in terms of $r$ and $theta$.
2. Rewrite the integrand in terms of $r$ and $theta$.
3. Change $d A$ into polar form.

Note: Most of the time, it is difficult to invert $d r$ and $d theta$.

Some important trig identities:
- $sin^2 theta + cos^2 theta = 1$
- $tan^2 theta + 1 = sec^2 theta$
- $sin 2 theta = 2 sin theta cos theta$
- $cos 2 theta = cos^2 theta - sin^2 theta$
- $sin^2 theta = 1/2 (1 - cos 2 theta)$
- $cos^2 theta = 1/2 (1 + cos 2 theta)$
