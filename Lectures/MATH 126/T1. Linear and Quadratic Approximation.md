## 4 Pillars of Calculus

1. Infinity
2. Approximations
3. Rate of change
4. Optimisation

---

First-degree Taylor Polynomial of $f$:
- Must match value of $f(b)$ at $b$: $T_1(b) = f(b)$
- Must match slope of $f(b)$ at $b$: $T_1 '(b) = f'(b)$
- Therefore, $T_1(x) = f(b)+ f'(b)(x - b)$

Second-degree Taylor Polynomial of $f$:
- Must match value of $f(b)$ at $b$: $T_2(b) = f(b)$
- Must match slope of $f(b)$ at $b$: $T_2 '(b) = f'(b)$
- Must match curvature of $f(b)$ at $b$: $T_2 ''(b) = f''(b)$

---

Let $t$ be in $[x, b]$;

$$ f(x) - f(b) = integral_b^x f'(t) d t $$

If we integrate by parts,
$u = f'(t)$
$d v = d t$
$d u = f''(t) d t$
$v = t - x$ (Any constant is a valid antiderivative, we choose $-x$)

$$ = [f'(t) (t - x)]_b^x - integral_b^x f''(t)(t - x) d t $$
$$ = -f'(b) (b - x) - integral_b^x f''(t)(t - x) d t $$
$$ = -f'(b)(x - b) + integral_b^x f''(t)(x - t) d t $$
$$ f(x) - f(b) - f'(b)(x - b) = integral_b^x f''(t)(x - t) d t $$
$$ f(x) - (f(b) + f'(b)(x - b)) = integral_b^x f''(t)(x - t) d t $$
$$ |f(x) - T_1(x)| = integral_b^x f''(t)(x - t) d t $$

Note: $|f(x) - T_1(x)|$ is the **error**!

$$ "Error" = |f(x) - T_1(x)| $$

Assume $|f''(t)| <= M$ for $b <= t <= x$:

$$ "Error" <= integral_b^x |f''(t)| (x - t) d t $$
$$ <= M integral_b^x (x - t) d t $$
$$ <= M [-(x - t)^2 / 2]_b^x $$
$$ <= M (x - b)^2 / 2 $$

---

Again...

$$ f(x) - (f(b) + f'(b)(x - b)) = integral_b^x f''(t)(x - t) d t $$

$u = f''(t)$
$d v = (x - t) d t$
$d u = f'''(t) d t$
$v = -(x - t)^2 / 2$

$$ = [-f''(t) (x - t)^2 / 2]_b^x + integral_b^x f'''(t) (x - t)^2 / 2 d t $$
$$ = f''(b) (x - b)^2 / 2 + integral_b^x f'''(t) (x - t)^2 / 2 d t $$
$$ f(x) - (f(b) + f'(b)(x - b) + f''(b) (x - b)^2 / 2) = integral_b^x f'''(t) (x - t)^2 / 2 d t $$

$$ "Error" = |f(x) - T_2(x)| $$
$$ <= integral_b^x |f'''(t)| (x - t)^2 / 2 d t $$
$$ <= M integral_b^x (x - t)^2 / 2 d t $$
$$ <= M [-(x - t)^3 / 6]_b^x d t $$
$$ <= M (x - b)^3 / 6 $$

---

> The **first Taylor polynomial** (or **linear approximation**) of $f(x)$ at $x = b$ is:
> $$ T_1(x) = f(b) + f'(b)(x - b) $$
> The **second Taylor polynomial** (or **quadratic approximation**) of $f(x)$ at $x = b$ is:
> $$ T_2(x) = f(b) + f'(b)(x - b) + f''(b)(x - b)^2 / 2 $$

$b$ is often called a **base point**.

> If $|f''(t)| <= M$ for any $t$ between $x$ and $b$, then the error $|T_1(x) - f(x)|$ is at most:
> $$ |T_1(x) - f(x)| <= M/2 (x - b)^2 $$
> If $|f'''(t)| <= M$ for any $t$ between $x$ and $b$, then the error $|T_2(x) - f(x)|$ is at most:
> $$ |T_2(x) - f(x)| <= M/6 (x - b)^6 $$

To find $M$, use the endpoint that makes the derivative the largest.
