**Physical weathering:** Processes breaking down rocks or sediment into small constituent fractions without changing the chemistry of the primary minerals.

**Chemical weathering:** Chemical reactions with primary minerals that change their mineralogy to a more stable compound.

Joints (weathered fractures) increase weathering because they increase the surface area of the rock. They also provide microenvironments for vegetation growth.

Three natural methods of weathering:
- **Frost wedging:** When water freezes, it expands, cracking the rock.
- **Thermal spalling:** Heat from forest fires affects a rock unevenly, causing outer layers of a rock to flake off.
- **Vegetation:** Plants growing and spreading roots through a rock.

**Hydrolosis:** Convert primary feldspars in rock to clay minerals such as kaolinite. Involves hydrogen ions derived from carbonic or other acids in the environment.

Karst landscape: Large pillars that are carved from erosion.

Stalactites ("hang on tight") grow down from ceilings, stalagmites grow up from the ground.

Felsic minerals tend to be more stable (resistant to weathering) than mafic minerals due to their strong $"Si" "O"_2$ bonds.

## Formation of sedimentary rock

1. Particles accumulate in basins.
2. Pressure closes gaps between individual rocks.
3. Water cements/glues rocks together.

Size of grains tells you energy of transporting agent (e.g. slight wind vs roaring river).

**Sortedness:** How similar the sizes of nearby rocks are. Low sortedness is indicative of glaciers.

Roundedness also tells you about the energy of the abrasion agent.

**Differential weathering:** The "glue" minerals can get weathered away in a composite, resulting in larger pebbles falling out.

Soil is created from a source rock, and is important for agriculture - retaining water and nutrients, neutralising acids, etc.

Weathering != erosion: Weathering is the physical alteration of a rock, erosion is the movement of a rock.

Soil forming factors (CLORPT):
- Climate
- Organisms
- Relief
- Parent material
- Time
