**Physical weathering:** Processes breaking down rocks or sediment into small constituent fractions without changing the chemistry of the primary minerals.

**Chemical weathering:** Chemical reactions with primary minerals that change their mineralogy to a more stable compound.

Joints (weathered fractures) increase weathering because they increase the surface area of the rock. They also provide microenvironments for vegetation growth.

Three natural methods of weathering:
- **Frost wedging:** When water freezes, it expands, cracking the rock.
- **Thermal spalling:** Heat from forest fires affects a rock unevenly, causing outer layers of a rock to flake off.
- **Vegetation:** Plants growing and spreading roots through a rock.
