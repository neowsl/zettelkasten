## Formation of the Earth

1. Space dust collides due to gravity, increasing internal temperature
2. Internal temperature rises enough to melt metals
3. Denser metals fall towards centre of Earth

We have never drilled to the centre of the Earth, but we have evidence for what we believe would be at the centre.

Earth has a density of $5.5 "g"/"cm"^3$.
- The Earth's crust has a density between 2.6 - 3.0
- The uppermost mantle is between 3.0 - 3.3
Therefore, the centre of the Earth must be made of a denser material than the outer layers.

We collect samples from meteorites to understand what materials were available to make up the Earth.

Magnetic field of Earth indicates convecting magnetic metals in Earth's core.

## Earthquakes

**P-waves:** Go *through* the Earth, think push-pull.
**S-waves:** Motion perpendicular to the direction of the wave.

The lithosphere (crust) is in isostatic equilibrium with the asthenosphere (mantle). Like a ferry that sinks when cars drive onto it, the crust "sinks" when there is more sediment deposited onto it.

The lithosphere grows in the oceans, but the Earth isn't growing, so the excess material gets buried into **subduction zones**.

## Plate tectonics

We can find rocks today in locations like Australia, India, South America, etc. that are evidence of glacial formations, supporting that the continents were once together at the South Pole.

We can also find animals and plants across continents, even though they don't swim.

Earthquakes caused by plates slipping over/under or past each other. Most are over/under, the San Andreas Fault is an instance of past.

Earth's **lithosphere** floats on top of the **asthenosphere**. The asthenosphere is *partially melted*, meaning it undergoes stuff like convection. Ocean basins in the lithosphere are *denser* than the composition of dry land, meaning they *sink* into the asthenosphere.

Magma (partial melt) is different in composition than the source rock, because minerals have different melting points.

Hot stuff rises, so it rises to the surface and pushes the surrounding topography apart.

The polarity of basalt flow flips... meaning that the Earth's magnetic poles reverses on a cycle.

At a subduction zone, material is scraped off like a cheese grater and thrusted upwards, creating mountain ranges.

Basalt is created from partial melt of the Earth's mantle. Andesite is created in subduction zones and is sometimes a mixture of different magma types.

**Porphyritic:** Having two sizes of crystals. The larger crystals are formed by earlier cooling, insulated under the surface of the Earth. The smaller crystals are formed by later, rapid cooling once the magma is ejected out of the Earth.
