Welcome everyone, I'm Neal 👋! This is the start of Weekly Typed, a weekly blog about fascinating topics in the world of computer science. Whether you're a battle-hardened developer or a student curious about CS, I'd like to share some inspiration and ideas in these miniature posts. At the very least, I hope to make your 15-minute coffee break seem a bit more productive to your boss.

You might be wondering about the name of this blog. "Weekly Typed" is a play on words. I type out this blog weekly, but it draws on the idea of "weak typing" in a programming context. Contrary to a strongly-typed language, a weakly-typed language allows data to be flexible, to change, to morph. That's what I'd like this blog to be: a dynamic motivator of fun conversations and ideas.

Though I have to admit, I am a huge fan of strongly-typed languages. My favourite language, Haskell, is the poster child for extreme strong typing. In Haskell, the type system is not just a safety net; it's almost like a second brain for the compiler. You can code invariants directly in the type system, essentially writing contracts with the compiler.

However, you might be wondering, "Aren't compiler checks just extra limitations on your code?" I disagree. Structure can be the foundation of beauty. I would argue that the Sacré-Cœur Basilica is much more creative and beautiful than a sand castle, even though sand is a more dynamic building material than stone.

Haskell's type system is even Turing complete! This means that you can encode arbitrary computation in the types themselves, which is still mind-boggling to me. Check the pinned comment for an article showing how Haskell’s type system alone can tackle a technical interview challenge!

If Haskell’s type system blows your mind, why not put it to the test? Advent of Code is coming up in a couple of months, and it's the perfect playground for functional programming challenges.
