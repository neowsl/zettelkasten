## 2026-02-06

$$ C equiv Q / (Delta V) $$

Where:
- $Q$ = total charge on the positive plate
- $Delta V$ = potential difference between the plates

$C$ tells us how much charge we can store for every volt of potential difference.
In other words, $C$ tells us how much charge we need to store to get a 1V potential difference.

$C$ is simply a ratio; **adding more charge to a capacitor doesn't change its capacitance.**

## 2026-02-09

$$ C = Q / (Delta V) = (epsilon_0 A) / d $$

**Capacitors in series:**

$$ C_"eq" = 1 / C_1 + 1 / C_2 $$
$$ Delta V_"eq" = Delta V_1 + Delta V_2 $$

**Capacitors in parallel:**

$$ C_"eq" = C_1 + C_2 $$

## 2026-02-11

If there is an insulator with dielectric constant $kappa (kappa >= 1)$, the E-field within the capacitor is reduced from $E_0$ to $E$:
$$ E = E_0 / kappa $$
And the capacitance is increased from $C_0$ to $C$:
$$ C = kappa C_0 $$
