## 2026-01-05

Objects can interact without direct contact
- Gravity
- Electrostatic interactions
- Magnetic interactions

Macroscopic objects contain **large** numbers of positive and negative point particles
- Positive = **protons**
- Negative = **electrons**

Charge of an object:
- Neutral: $N_+ = N_-$
- Positive: $N_+ > N_-$
- Negative: $N_+ < N_-$

$$ q = N_+ e - N_- e $$

Where $e = 1.619 times 10^(-19) C$ (coulombs)

In conductors, charges move freely
In insulators, charges mostly stay in place

See: [Electroscope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electroscope)

Air is a very poor conducting pathway

## 2026-01-07

Neutral objects attract positive/negative objects! Only neutral : neutral does nothing.

Magnets do not inherently hold net charge, nor are they inherently polarised. However, they can be charged like any object, and behave like any other metal object.

Electrical forces behave the same as mechanical forces. I.e. Newton's laws still hold.

### Charging by induction

If two metal objects are in contact, and a charged object is brought near one, and then the two metal objects are separated, the two metal objects will be charged.

### Discharging

If you connect two conductive objects with a conducting wire, their charges will be averaged based on mass. I.e., if you want to discharge an object, you can connect it to the Earth, essentially dissipating all of the charge.

Note: Charged != polarised

### Coulomb's Law

$$ F_"1 on 2" = F_"2 on 1" = (k |q_1| |q_2|) / (r^2_12) $$

Where:
- Objects 1 and 2 are *point particles* (infinitesimally small volume, but has mass and charge)
- $q_1$ and $q_2$ are in Coulombs
- $r_12$ is in metres
- $k = 9 times 10^9 (N m^2) / c^2 = 1 / (4 pi epsilon_0)$

Note: This applies to *microscopic*, not *macroscopic* objects. Otherwise, a zero-charge object would experience 0 force.

## 2026-01-09

**Superposition**: If there are many charged particles,

$$ arrow(F)_("net" A) = sum_i arrow(F)_(i A) $$

### Electric field (E-field)

If every point in a space can be characterised by a *number*, it is a **scalar field**.

If every point in a space can be characterised by a *number* and a *direction*, it is a **vector field**.

$g h$: **potential**

$$ F = q E $$

Where $E$ is the strength of the electric field (Newtons/Coulomb.

## 2026-01-12

If we have two point charges $q_0$ and $q_t$,

Let $arrow(r)_(0t)$ be a unit vector pointing from $q_0$ to $q_t$.

$$ arrow(F)_(0t) = (k q_0 q_t) / (r_(0t)^2) arrow(r)_(0t) $$

Thus,

$$ arrow(F) = q arrow(E) $$

### Field lines

- Start on positive charges, end on negative charges
- If using lines, density of lines represents strength of field
- If using vectors, length of vectors represents strength of field
