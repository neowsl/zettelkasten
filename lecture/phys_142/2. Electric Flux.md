## 2026-01-21

**Electric flux**: The amount of E-field that passes through a surface. Maximum flux is when $E$ is perpendicular to $A$.

$$ Phi = arrow(E) dot arrow(A) = integral arrow(E) dot d arrow(A) $$

## 2026-01-23

Flux is proportional to the number of field lines; electric field is proportional to the number of field lines per unit area.

### Closed surfaces

Imaginary closed surfaces are called **Gaussian surfaces**.

A closed surface over a dipole has net zero flux.

Gaussian surfaces *are not real*. You can imagine a surface of any shape at any location, and it shouldn't break the physics. (Metaphor: a **system** from PHYS141)

No charge outside a Gaussian surface can affect the net flux of that surface. However, it *can* affect the E-field of points in the surface.

### Gauss's Law

$$ integral.cont arrow(E) dot d arrow(A) = Q_"enc" / epsilon_0 $$

Left-hand side: Flux
$Q_"enc"$: Charge inside the Gaussian surface

#### Symmetries

Spherical charge distribution -> Use spherical Gaussian surface
Cylindrical charge distribution -> Use cylindrical Gaussian surface (curved part is $perp$ field lines)
Planar charge distribution -> Use cylindrical Gaussian surface (flat faces are $perp$ field lines)
