## 2026-04-20: Modular Arithmetic

> **Definition: "b divides a"**
> For $a$, $b$ (usually with $b != 0$),
> $$ b | a := exists q (a = q b) $$

> **Division Theorem**
> For $a$, $b$ with $b > 0$, there exist *unique* integers $q$, $r$ with $0 <= r < b$ such that $a = q b + r$.

> **Definition: "a is congruent to b modulo m"**
> For $a$, $b$, $m$ with $m > 0$,
> $$ a equiv_m b := m | (a - b) $$

## 2026-04-22: Euclid's Algorithm

$$ gcd(a, b) = gcd(d, a mod b) $$
$$ gcd(a, 0) = a $$

> **Bezout's Theorem**
> If $a$ and $b$ are positive integers, then there exist integers $s$ and $t$ such that
> $$ gcd(a, b) = s a + t b $$

To find $s$ and $t$, use Euclid's Algorithm going down while keeping track of a tableau. Then solve for every remainder, and back-substitute to get to larger numbers.

> Let $0 <= a, b < m$. Then, $b$ is the **multiplicative inverse** of $a$ (modulo $m$) iff $a b equiv_m 1$.
