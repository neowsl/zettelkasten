## 2026-06-01: Reductions

> **Rice's Theorem**
> Any *non-trivial* property of the **input-output behaviour** of Java programs is undecidable.

"Non-trivial" means there is at least one program with the property and one program without it.

Programming languages equal in power to Java, C, etc. are called **Turing-complete**.

Some surprising languages that are Turing-complete:
- Excel macros
- TypeScript's type system
- Conway's Game of Life
- Magic: The Gathering

## 2026-06-05: Applications of Number Theory

**Satisfiability (SAT) problems**: Is there any set of inputs to a proposition that produces T?
- Useful for logic problems, e.g. verifying hardware (by XOR'ing two circuits and looking for Ts)

### P vs. NP

**P** = Problems solvable by Turing machines in polynomial time
**NP** = Problems solvable by *nondeterministic* Turing machines in polynomial time

Essentially, nondeterministic TMs can explore *all possible solutions* of a problem in "parallel", and the parallelisation aspect is why NP problems are difficult to solve. This highlights a key characteristic of NP-class problems, that they are *verifiable* in polynomial time by a TM.

- All NP problems can be reduced to SAT, and SAT can be reduced to many other problems

### $lambda$-calculus

Syntax:
$$
S -> & V \
   | & lambda V . S \
   | & S S \
   | & (S)
$$
$$ V -> x | y | z | ... $$

If all variables are bound, every expression evaluates to a function.

When are two results "the same"?
- Variable renaming ($alpha$-conversion): $lambda x . E equiv lambda y . E [y \/ x]$
- Call unwrapping ($eta$-conversion): $lambda x . f x equiv f$

Only computational step is a function call:

$$ (lambda x . E_1) E_2 equiv E_1 [E_2 \/ x] $$

### Typed $lambda$-calculus

Syntax:
$$
S -> & T \
   | & T -> S
$$
$$
T -> & ZZ \
