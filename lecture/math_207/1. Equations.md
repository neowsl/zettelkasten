> A **differential equation** is an equation involving derivatives.
> An equation that describes some physical phenomena is often called a **mathematical model**.

Examples:
- Population growth: $(d p(t)) / (d t)$
- Temperature change: $(d T) / (d t) = k (T - T_"ext")$
- Newton's 2nd law: $m dot (d^2 x) / (d t) = F$
- Oceanside waves

$(d y) / (d x) = ln(x)$ <- ordinary differential equation
$(partial^2 u) / (partial x^2) = c^2 (partial^2 u^2) / (partial t^2)$ <- partial differential equation (won't cover this quarter)

Order of an equation = the highest order derivative in an equation

> *Example*
> A pond initially contains 1,000,000gal of water and 500g of an undesirable chemical. Water containing 0.01g of this chemical per gallon flows into the pond at a rate of 300gal/h. The mixture flows out at the same rate, so the amount of water in the pond remains constant. Assume that the chemical is uniformly distributed throughout the pond. Find a differential equation for the amount of chemical in the pond at any time $t$.

Let $A(t)$ = the amount (grams) of chemical in the pond at time $t$ (hours)

We want to find $(d A) / (d t)$, the rate of change of amount of chemical in the pool at time $t$.

This will be amount added per hour - amount removed per hour

Chemical is added at $300"gal"/h dot 0.01g/"gal" = 3g/h$

To find the rate at which the chemical is removed, we know that the concentration of chemical in the pool at any time $t$ is $300 dot A(t) / 1000000 g/h$
