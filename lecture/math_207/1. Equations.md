## 2026-01-05

> A **differential equation** is an equation involving derivatives.
> An equation that describes some physical phenomena is often called a **mathematical model**.

Examples:
- Population growth: $(d p(t)) / (d t)$
- Temperature change: $(d T) / (d t) = k (T - T_"ext")$
- Newton's 2nd law: $m dot (d^2 x) / (d t) = F$
- Oceanside waves

$(d y) / (d x) = ln(x)$ <- ordinary differential equation
$(partial^2 u) / (partial x^2) = c^2 (partial^2 u^2) / (partial t^2)$ <- partial differential equation (won't cover this quarter)

Order of an equation = the highest order derivative in an equation

> *Example*
> A pond initially contains 1,000,000gal of water and 500g of an undesirable chemical. Water containing 0.01g of this chemical per gallon flows into the pond at a rate of 300gal/h. The mixture flows out at the same rate, so the amount of water in the pond remains constant. Assume that the chemical is uniformly distributed throughout the pond. Find a differential equation for the amount of chemical in the pond at any time $t$.

Let $A(t)$ = the amount (grams) of chemical in the pond at time $t$ (hours)

We want to find $(d A) / (d t)$, the rate of change of amount of chemical in the pool at time $t$.

This will be amount added per hour - amount removed per hour

Chemical is added at $300"gal"/h dot 0.01g/"gal" = 3g/h$

To find the rate at which the chemical is removed, we know that the concentration of chemical in the pool at any time $t$ is $300 dot A(t) / 1000000 g/h$

## 2026-01-07

The 500g from above is called an **initial condition**. The type of problem is called an **initial value problem**. We can represent this as:

$$ A(0) = 500g $$

> A solution to a differential equation is a *function* that satisfies the equation.

> *Example*
> A ball of mass 2kg is thrown upward from the ground with an initial velocity $v = 3 m/s$.
> 1. Find an equation for the velocity as a function of time and solve the equation.
> 2. Find an equation for the altitude of the object as a function of time and solve the equation.
> 3. Find an equation for the velocity as a function of altitude.
> 4. The air is acting on the ball with an air resistance force proportional to the velocity. The proportionality constant is $k = 3 "Ns"/m$. Find an equation for the velocity as a function of time. Find a solution to the differential equation in the form $v(t) = A + B e^(C t)$.

Let $v(t)$ = the velocity of the object (upwards is positive)
$v(0) = 3 m/s$
$m dot (d v) / (d t) = -m g$
$(d v) / (d t) = -g$
$v(t) = -g t + C$

Therefore, $v(t) = -g t + 3$.

---

Let $z(t)$ = the altitude of the object
$z(0) = 0$
$(d z) / (d t) = v(t) = -g t + 3$
$z(t) = (-g t^2) / 2 + 3t + C$

Therefore, $z(t) = (-g t^2) / 2 + 3t$.

---

$v(0) = 3$
$m dot (d v(t)) / (d t) = -m g - 3v(t)$
If we trust the exercise, we know $v(t) = A + B e^(C t)$.
$(d v) / (d t) = B C e^(C t)$
$2 dot B C e^(C t) = -2g - 3(A + B e^(C t))$
$(2 B C + 3 B) e^(C t) = -2g - 3A$
How can we make the left-side exponential function into the right-side constant function?
$2 B C + 3 B$ must be 0.
$B = 0$ or $C = -3/2$
$B = 0$ is impossible in this problem.
$-2g - 3A = 0$ -> $A = -(2g) / 3$
$v(t) = -(2g)/3 + B e^(-(3t)/2)$
Recall that $v(0) = 3$

Therefore, $v(t) = -(2g)/3 + (3 + (2g)/3) e^(-(3t)/2)$.
