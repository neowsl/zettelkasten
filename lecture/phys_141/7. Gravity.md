Two objects with masses $m_1$ and $m_2$ attract each other with

$$ F_21 = F_12 = (G m_1 m_2) / (r^2) $$

Where $G = 6.67 times 10^(-11) (N m^2) / (k g^2)$

Near the surface of the Earth,

$$ F_(E "obj") = (G m_"obj" m_E) / (r_E^2) = m_"obj" g $$

Note that the $m$ in $F = m a$ and the $m$ in gravitational force are actually not the same! The former is "inertial mass", the latter is "gravitational mass". But up to now, they appear to be identical.

**Principle of equivalence**: Inertial and gravitational mass are equal.

For an object of mass $m$ and the Earth,

$$ U(r) = - (G m_E m) / r $$

However, as observers on the surface of the Earth,
1. The distance we drop something from is negligible to the radius of the Earth
2. The curvature of the Earth is essentially zero
