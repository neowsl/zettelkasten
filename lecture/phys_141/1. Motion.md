> **Kinematics**
> A pure description of motion (no causes, just description)

> **Position**
> Where something is at an instant in time
> $$ arrow(r) $$
> I.e. a vector from the origin to the location of the object.

$arrow(i)$, $arrow(j)$, and $arrow(k)$ are unit vectors in the xyz coordinate system.

> **Displacement**
> $$ Delta arrow(r) equiv arrow(r)_2 - arrow(r)_1 $$

> **Time interval**
> $$ Delta t = t_2 - t_1 $$

> **Velocity**
> $$ arrow(v)_"avg" equiv (Delta arrow(r)) / (Delta t) $$

Recall:
$$ arrow(r)_2 = arrow(r)_1 + Delta arrow(r) = arrow(r)_1 + arrow(v)_"avg" Delta t $$

Non-constant velocity is indicated by a non-constant first derivative on the position graph of an object.

> **Instantaneous velocity**
> Let $Delta arrow(x) arrow d arrow(x)$
> $Delta t arrow d t$
> $$ arrow(v) equiv (d arrow(x)) / (d t) $$
> $d t$ must be small enough that $arrow(v)$ looks constant.

So far, we have used the **Earth frame** (a.k.a. **lab frame**) - a reference frame fixed to the Earth. An *inertial* reference frame.

Some things to consider:
- The velocity of an object relative to itself is 0.
- $arrow(v)_"AB" = -arrow(v)_"BA"$
- $arrow(v)_"AC" = arrow(v)_"AB" + arrow(v)_"BC"$

## 2025-10-10

### Kinematics of circular motion

![[1. Motion 2025-10-10 09.45.34.excalidraw]]

In an interval $Delta t$, the bug moves a distance $arrow(s)$:
$$ arrow(v) equiv (Delta arrow(s)) / (Delta t) $$

Angular velocity:
$$ omega equiv (Delta theta) / (Delta t) $$

Tangential velocity:
$$ arrow(v) equiv (r Delta theta) / (Delta t) = r omega $$

Centripetal acceleration:
$$ a_perp = a_"radial" = a_"rad" equiv arrow(v)^2 / r $$
$$ a_perp equiv r omega^2 $$

#### Increasing speed

$$ a_"tangential" = (d arrow(v)) / (d t) = (d r omega) / (d t) = r (d omega) / (d t) $$
$$ alpha = (d omega) / (d t) = (d^2 theta) / (d t^2) $$
$$ a_"tan" equiv r alpha $$

### Equations

$Delta theta$ = displacement
$omega equiv (Delta theta) / (Delta t)$ = angular velocity
$alpha equiv (Delta omega) / (Delta t)$ = angular acceleration

The transition point between speeding up and slowing down is where acceleration = 0.
