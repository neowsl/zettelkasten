> **Kinematics**
> A pure description of motion (no causes, just description)

> **Position**
> Where something is at an instant in time
> $$ arrow(r) $$
> I.e. a vector from the origin to the location of the object.

$arrow(i)$, $arrow(j)$, and $arrow(k)$ are unit vectors in the xyz coordinate system.

> **Displacement**
> $$ Delta arrow(r) equiv arrow(r)_2 - arrow(r)_1 $$

> **Time interval**
> $$ Delta t = t_2 - t_1 $$

> **Velocity**
> $$ arrow(v)_"avg" equiv (Delta arrow(r)) / (Delta t) $$

Recall:
$$ arrow(r)_2 = arrow(r)_1 + Delta arrow(r) = arrow(r)_1 + arrow(v)_"avg" Delta t $$

Non-constant velocity is indicated by a non-constant first derivative on the position graph of an object.

> **Instantaneous velocity**
> Let $Delta arrow(x) arrow d arrow(x)$
> $Delta t arrow d t$
> $$ arrow(v) equiv (d arrow(x)) / (d t) $$
> $d t$ must be small enough that $arrow(v)$ looks constant.

## 2025-09-29

$$ Delta arrow(s) equiv arrow(s)_f - arrow(s)_i arrow.double arrow(s)_f = arrow(s)_i + Delta arrow(s) $$

> *Reminder*
> $$ arrow(A) = A hat(i) $$

$$ v_"avg" equiv (Delta s) / (Delta t) = (s_f - s_i) / (t_f - t_i) $$

Slope of $s$ vs. $t$ = velocity
Area under graph of $v$ vs. $t$ = displacement

Average velocity is the constant velocity required to achieve the same displacement in the same time interval.

For a uniformly increasing velocity starting at 0, $v_"avg" = 1/2 v_f$.

For a uniformly increasing velocity (not necessarily starting at 0), $Delta s = 1/2 (v_f - v_i)(t_f - t_i) + v_i (t_f - t_i)$.
Or, $Delta s = 1/2 Delta v Delta t + v_i Delta t$.

$$ arrow(a)_"avg" = (Delta arrow(v)) / (Delta t) = (arrow(v)_f - arrow(v)_i) / (Delta t) $$
