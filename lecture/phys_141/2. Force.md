## 2025-10-10

> Forces are pushes and pulls that arise from *interactions*.

Interactions: One object exerts a force on another and vice versa.

Forces are vector quantities.

Can be **contact** (e.g. friction) or **long-range** (e.g. gravity/electromagnetic).

> **Gravitational force**
> Interaction between 2 objects that have mass.

Near the Earth, all objects of mass are attracted to the Earth.

$arrow(F)_"EB"^"grav"$: The *gravitational* force *by* the Earth *on* the box.

$$ |arrow(F)_"EB"^"grav" = m g $$

Where the gravitational field strength $g = 9.8 "N" / "kg"$.

Spring, tension, and normal forces are all $perp$ to the surface of contact.
These forces require some compression or extension of (at least) one of the objects.

## 2025-10-13

### Types of forces

1. Gravitational forces
	- Attraction between any two objects with mass
	- Doesn't require contact
2. Spring, tension, and normal forces
	- Require contact
	- Perpendicular to surface of contact
	- Require some deformation of the objects
3. Frictional forces
	- Require contact
	- Parallel to surface of contact
	- Require some deformation of the objects
	- STATIC
		- If *no* relative motion of the objects
		- Opposite the direction of *impending* motion
		- *Upper limit* of magnitude depends on
			- Type of surface
			- Normal forces they exert on each other
	- KINETIC
		- *If* relative motion of the objects
		- Opposite the direction of motion
		- *Magnitude* depends on
			- Type of surface
			- Normal forces they exert on each other

### Newton's first law (N1)

An object at rest remains at rest; an object moving with constant velocity continues to move with constant velocity in the absence of a net force.

### Newton's second law (N2)

$$ arrow(F)_"net, obj" = m_"obj" arrow(a)_"obj" $$

Or, $arrow(a) = arrow(F)_"net" / m$

If multiple forces are acting on $"obj"$:

$$ arrow(F)_"net, obj" = sum_i arrow(F)_"i, obj" $$

### Friction

$$ f_12^s lt.eq mu_s N_12 $$
$$ f_12^k = mu_k N_12 $$

$mu_s$: Coefficient of static friction
$mu_k$: Coefficient of kinetic friction

Note: Static friction is an upper limit; kinetic friction is an equal-to.
