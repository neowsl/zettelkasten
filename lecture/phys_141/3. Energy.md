## 2025-10-22

3 conserved quantities in this course:
- Energy $E$
- Momentum $p$
- Angular momentum $L$

Energy can only change if it is "balanced" with the energy in another system.

If we observe that the energy of a system changes, we know that the energy of some other system has changed in exactly the opposite way.
$$ Delta E_"sys 1" = -Delta E_"sys 2" $$

If the system does not interact with any other systems, $Delta E = 0$.

We can minimise interactions to make the energy of a system constant.

$$ Delta E_"sys" = Delta K + Delta U + Delta E_"th" = W_"ext" $$

A **system** is any collection of objects we can mentally isolate from its surroundings.

## 2025-10-24

A system that can be treated as a particle (meaning it has no internal structure or interactions that matter) has *only KINETIC energy*.

$$ K equiv 1/2 m v^2 $$
$$ W equiv F dot Delta x $$

Where $arrow(a) dot arrow(b) = |arrow(a)| |arrow(b)| cos theta$. It is a scalar value.

If $arrow(a)$ and $arrow(b)$ are:
- Parallel $parallel$, then $arrow(a) dot arrow(b) = |arrow(a)| |arrow(b)|$.
- Parallel, then $arrow(a) dot arrow(b) = |arrow(a)| |arrow(b)|$.

For a particle,

$$ K_f - K_i = F Delta s $$

---

If the system cannot be treated as a particle,

$Delta x$ is the displacement of the point of application of the force.

In a scenario where a rubber ball is bouncing on the floor, there is actually *no work* done by the floor on the ball! This is because the displacement of the point of application of force (the bottom of the ball while it is in contact with the ground) does not change, so $Delta x = 0$, and therefore $W = 0$.

The rubber ball cannot be modelled as a particle since it *changes shape*.

## 2025-10-29

$$ W = arrow(F) Delta x = integral arrow(F)(x) d x $$

Given that the force on a spring is $-k(s - s_0)$:

$$ W_"spring obj" = -k integral_(s_0)^(s_f) (s - s_"eq") d s $$

Letting $v = s - s_"eq"$, we get:

$$ W_"spring obj" = -k integral_(s_0 - s_"eq")^(s_f - s_"eq") v d v $$
$$ = -k lr(v^2/2 ])_(s_0 - s_"eq")^(s_f - s_"eq")$$
$$ = -k/2 [(s_f - s_"eq")^2 - (s_0 - s_"eq")^2] $$

If we set $s_"eq" = 0$:

$$ W_"spring obj" = -k/2 [s_f^2 - s_0^2] $$

### Thermal energy and dissipative forces

**Thermal energy** is random motion at a molecular level. It will be warmer as a result of molecular movement. This energy is difficult to make use of.

**Dissipative forces** always increase thermal energy.

Thermal energy causes internal structure changes, meaning that an object with thermal energy cannot be treated as a particle. (Remember, particles can only be used when the only energy is kinetic.)

$$ Delta W = Delta K + Delta E_"th" $$

For the block sliding across the floor with friction:

$$ Delta K = -f^k Delta x $$
$$ W_"on block" = -f^k Delta x + Delta E_"th" $$

Since $Delta E_"th"$ is not 0,

$$ W_"on block" != -f^k Delta x $$

Think about the block as a "scrub brush": the bottom face moves with many many different forces being applied. Therefore, we *cannot* calculate work associated with dissipative forces.

Thus, for the block-floor scenario, we should choose our system as the block + some portion of the floor. This way, $W_"on system" = 0$.

$$ Delta K + Delta E_"thermal block" + Delta E_"thermal floor" = 0 $$

Or, since $Delta K = -f^k Delta x$,

$$ Delta E_"thermal block+floor" = f^k Delta x $$

## 2025-10-31

**Potential energy**: $U$
- Depends on *configuration*

If an interaction is internal to a system, it shows up in the $Delta E$ in $Delta E = W$.

$Delta E_"thermal"$: Depends on the path of the object
$Delta U$: Doesn't depend on the path of the object (*path-independent*)

### Elastic potential energy

$U^"elastic"$ or $U^"el"$: A spring's potential energy

$$ Delta U^"el" = 1/2 K(s_f^2 - s_i^2) $$
 (If $s_"eq" = 0$)
 
### Gravitational potential energy

$$ Delta K = -m g h $$
$$ Delta U^"grav" = m g Delta y $$

## 2025-11-03

$$ K_i + U_(G i) + U_("sp" i) + W = K_f + U_(G f) + U_("sp" f) $$

Note that the $y$ in potential energy does not need to be relative to the floor; any starting height can be used (meaning $U$ could be negative!)

*Always* correct to say $Delta E = W_"ext"$.

Equilibrium is always the point of lowest total potential energy. Any deviation from equilibrium will increase total potential energy (make more positive).

$$ [k] = N/m $$
