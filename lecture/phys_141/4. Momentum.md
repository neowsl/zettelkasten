## 2025-11-05

Second of the three conserved quantities ($E$, $arrow(p)$, $arrow(L)$).

> **Momentum**
> $$ arrow(p) equiv m arrow(v) $$

Unlike $E$, $arrow(p)$ is a vector. $arrow(p)$ also cannot be transformed.

$$ K = arrow(p)^2 / (2m) $$

Unlike $E$, momentum can only change via transfers.

$$ arrow(F)_"net" = (d arrow(p)) / (d t) $$
$$ Delta arrow(p) = integral arrow(F) d t $$

## 2025-11-07

Like $E$, the momentum of a system is the sum of the momenta of its parts.

$$ arrow(p)_"sys" = sum_i arrow(p)_i = sum_i m_i arrow(v)_i $$

Collisions and explosions are two sides of the same coin.

If we can ignore other interactions during a collision,
- $arrow(p)_f = arrow(p)_i$
- $E_f = E_i$
- But $K_f$ might not necessarily $= K_i$

### 3 categories of collisions

1. **Perfectly inelastic**: Maximum irreversible conversion of $K$ to other forms.
	- $arrow(p)_f = arrow(p)_i$, but objects *stick together* and move as one.
2. **Inelastic**: Irreversible conversion of $K$ to some other form.
	- $arrow(p)_f = arrow(p)_i$
3. **Elastic**: Temporary, reversible conversion of $K$ to some other form.
	- The term *elastic* comes from the idea that you could have a spring in the middle of the collision.
	- $arrow(p)_f = arrow(p)_i$ *and* $K_f = K_i$

$$ Delta arrow(p) = -Delta arrow(p)_"ext" $$

## 2025-11-10

For elastic collisions of a stationary target:

$$ v_(1f) = (m_1 - m_2) / (m_1 + m_2) v_(1i) $$

$$ v_(2f) = (2m_1) / (m_1 + m_2) v_(1i) $$

1. $m_1 = m_2$:
	- $v_(1f) approx 0$
	- $v_(2f) approx v_(1f)$
2. $m_1 >> m_2$:
	- $v_(1f) approx v_(1i)$
	- $v_(2f) approx 2v_(1i)$
3. $m_1 << m_2$:
	- $v_(1f) approx -v_(1i)$
	- $v_(2f) approx 0$

---

Recall that $arrow(v)_(A B)$ represents the velocity of object $A$ in a reference frame in which object B is at rest (the "rest frame" of $B$).

$$ arrow(v)_(A B) equiv arrow(v)_(A C) + arrow(v)_(B C) $$

You can deduce the type of collision from the relative velocity of $A$ to $B$:
- $|arrow(v)_(A B)|^"after" < |arrow(v)_(A B)|^"before"$: inelastic
- $|arrow(v)_(A B)|^"after" = 0$: perfectly inelastic
- $|arrow(v)_(A B)|^"after" = |arrow(v)_(A B)|^"before"$: elastic

### Centre of mass

For an object of two points:
$$ x_"cm" = (m_1 x_1 + m_2 x_2) / (m_1 + m_2) $$

If we choose $x = 0$ at the centre of mass (cm):
$$ x_1 / x_2 = m_2 / m_1 $$

$$ arrow(v)_"cm" = (arrow(p)_"sys") / M_"sys" $$

To an observer moving with 0 velocity relative to the centre of mass of a system, the momentum of the system is 0. If the collision is perfectly inelastic, all kinetic energy will be reduced to 0 after the collision.
