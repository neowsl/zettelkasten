## 2025-11-14

For a rotating rigid body of a fixed axis:

$$ K = sum_i 1/2 m_i arrow(v)_i^2 = sum_i 1/2 m_i (r_i omega)^2 = sum_i 1/2 m_i r_i^2 omega^2 $$

If we define $I = sum_i m_i r_i^2$,

$$ K = 1/2 I omega^2 $$

Where $I$ is the "moment of inertia".

For combined translational and rotational motion:

$$ K = 1/2 M_"sys" v_"CM"^2 + 1/2 I omega^2 $$

## 2025-11-17

Just as [[4. Momentum|momentum]] is the product of mass times velocity, **angular momentum** is the product of moment of inertia times angular momentum.

For rotations that are *not* about the centre of mass:

$$ I_A = I_"CM" + M d_A^2 $$

This is called the **parallel axis theorem**.

$I_"CM"$: Moment of inertia about the centre of mass
$I_A$: Moment of inertia about the axis of rotation

**Torque**: A force that changes angular motion

$$ arrow(tau)_A equiv arrow(r)_A times arrow(F) $$

## 2025-11-19

$$ arrow(tau)_"total" equiv sum_i arrow(r)_(i A) times arrow(F)_i $$

Note that this is not simply the average torque.

**Right-hand rule**: To find the direction of $arrow(A) times arrow(B)$,
- Point index finger in direction of $arrow(A)$
- Point middle finger in direction of $arrow(B)$
- Cross product is direction of thumb

See also: [[12.4 Cross Product]]

$$ arrow(A) times arrow(B) = arrow(A) times (arrow(B)_perp + arrow(B)_parallel) = arrow(A) times arrow(B)_perp + arrow(A) times arrow(B)_parallel = arrow(A) times arrow(B) perp = A B sin theta $$

## 2025-11-21

Just as $a_"CM" = F / m$,

$$ alpha_"net" = tau_"net" / I $$

**Force couple**: Equal and opposite forces on parallel lines of action. Do not produce translational motion but do produce rotational motion.

The only thing that matters for a force couple is the relative distance between the lines of action! Their absolute locations on the object does not matter.

Static equilibrium:
- $arrow(a) = 0$
- $arrow(alpha) = 0$
- $arrow(F)_"net" = 0$
- $arrow(tau)_"net" = 0$
