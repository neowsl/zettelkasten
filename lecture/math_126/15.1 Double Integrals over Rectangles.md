**Functions of one variable:**
Variables: $y = f(x)$
Integration: $integral_a^b f(x) d x$
Integration Region: Interval $[a, b]$

**Functions of two variables:**
Variables: $z = f(x, y)$
Integration: $integral.double_R f(x, y) d A$
Integration Region: Region $R$ in $bb(R)^2$

$R$ will be divided into a grid, with **sample points** $(x_(i j)^*, y_(i j)^*)$.

> **Double integral**
> The **double integral** of $f(x, y)$ over the rectangle $R$ is:
> $$ integral.double_R f(x, y) d A = lim_(m, n arrow infinity) sum_(j = 1)^n sum_(i = 1)^m f(x_(i j)^*, y_(i j)^*) Delta A $$

Observations:
1. If $f(x, y) >= 0$, then $integral.double_R f(x, y) d A$ gives us the *volume* of the solid above $R$ and below $z = f(x, y)$.
2. If $f(x, y) < 0$, then we have "negative volume".
3. If you want the volume below $f(x, y)$ and above $g(x, y)$, calculate $integral.double_R (f(x, y) - g(x, y)) d A$.

## Fabini's Theorem

$$ integral.double_R f(x, y) d A = integral_a^b integral_c^d f(x, y) d y d x = integral_c^d integral_a^b f(x, y) d x d y $$

Warning: Some integrals will *require* a switch of order.
