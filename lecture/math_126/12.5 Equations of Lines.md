> The two pieces of information you need to determine a line in 3D are:
> 1. A point on the line
> 2. A direction vector

![[12.5 Equations of Lines 2025-10-03 12.53.09.excalidraw]]

Forms:
1. Vector form
$$ arrow(r) = arrow(r)_0 + t arrow(v) $$
2. Parametric form
$$ x = x_0 + t a $$
$$ y = y_0 + t b $$
$$ z = z_0 + t c $$
3. Symmetric form
$$ (x - x_0) / a = (y - y_0) / b = (z - z_0) / c $$

> *Example*
> a. Find a set of parametric equations for the line passing through the points $P(1, 0, 3)$ and $Q(2, -1, 4)$.

$x = 1 + t$
$y = -t$
$y = 3 + t$

> b. Find a vector parametrization for the line that passes through $P(3, 1, 0)$ and is parallel to the line $arrow(r) (t) = (hat(i) - hat(j)) + t hat(k)$.

Notice that only the $hat(k)$ matters; the $hat(i) - hat(j)$ is simply a point on the second line. $t hat(k) = t angle.l 0, 0, 1 angle.r$.

$arrow(r) (t) = angle.l 3, 1, 0 angle.r + t angle.l 0, 0, 1 angle.r$

> c. Find a set of parametric equations for the line that passes through $P(2, -2, 3)$ and is perpendicular to the $x z$-plane.

$x = 2$
$y = -2 + t$
$z = 3$

Note how $y$ is the only component that changes when perpendicular to the $x z$ plane.

## Equations of a plane

Recall that we need:
1. A point on plane $arrow(r_0)$
2. A normal (orthogonal) vector $arrow(n)$

$arrow(n) = angle.l a, b, c angle.r$
$arrow(r)_0 = angle.l x_0, y_0, z_0 angle.r$
$arrow(r) = angle.l x, y, z angle.r$

$arrow(r)$ defines any point on the plane.

![[12.5 Equations of Lines 2025-10-06 13.01.03.excalidraw]]

Equation of a plane:
$$ a(x - x_0) + b(y - y_0) + c(z - z_0) = 0 $$
or
$$ a x + b y + c z = a x_0 + b y_0 + c z_0 $$

## Plane intersection

> Find the line of intersection of the two planes $x - 3y + z = 0$ and $-x + 4y = 4$.

1. Find a normal vector for both planes
2. The cross product of those two vectors will be the intersection of the two planes
3. Find a point $(x, y, z)$ that satisfies both equations (you'll need to make a decision for at least one of the variables)
