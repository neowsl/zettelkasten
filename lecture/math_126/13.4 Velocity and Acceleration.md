$$ arrow(F)(t) = m arrow(a)(t) $$

> **Tangential component**
> The **tangential component** $a_T$ and the **normal component** $a_N$ of acceleration $arrow(a)(t)$ is:
> $$ a_T = cos theta |arrow(a)| (|arrow(v)|) / (|arrow(v)|) = (arrow(a) dot arrow(v)) / (|arrow(v)|) $$
> $$ a_T = sin theta |arrow(a)| (|arrow(v)|) / (|arrow(v)|) = (|arrow(a) times arrow(v)|) / (|arrow(v)|) $$

> Also, the **unit tangent vector $T$** and the **unit normal vector $N$** of a vector function $arrow(r)(t)$ are:
> $$ arrow(T)(t) = (arrow(r)'(t)) / (|arrow(r)'(t)|) = (arrow(v)(t)) / (|arrow(v)(t)|) $$
> $$ arrow(N)(t) = (arrow(T)'(t)) / (|arrow(T)'(t)|) $$

$$ arrow(a)(t) = a_T arrow(T)(t) + a_N arrow(N)(t) $$

Note:
- $a_T$ measures changes in speed.
	- $a_T > 0$: Speeding up
	- $a_T = 0$: Constant speed
	- $a_T < 0$: Slowing down
- $a_N$ measures changes in direction.
	- $a_N > 0$: Turning
	- $a_N = 0$: Not turning

When calculating $arrow(N)(t)$, find $arrow(T)'(t)$ first. If $t$ is given, plug it in now; it'll make the calculations easier. Then, find the magnitude of the resultant vector and divide.
