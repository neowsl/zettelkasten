$(d f) / (d x)$: Derivative of $f$ with respect to $x$.
$(partial  f) / (partial x)$: Partial derivative of $f$ with respect to $x$.

> For a function $f(x, y)$, to find the partial derivatives $f_x$ and $f_y$:
> $f_x$: Treat $y$ as constant, let $x$ vary.
> $f_y$: Treat $x$ as constant, let $y$ vary.

> If $f(x, y) = y^3 - 3y x^2 - 3y^2 - 3x^2 + 1$, find $f_x$ and $f_y$. Then find when $f_x = f_y = 0$ simultaneously.

$(0, 0), (0, 2), (sqrt(3), -1), (-sqrt(3), -1)$

**Clairout's Theorem**: In the majority of functions, $f_(x y) = f_(y x)$.

$partial / (partial x) (...)$ = "Take derivative partially with respect to x".

## Level curves

The $k$-value indicates the "height" of the curve at that point. $(partial f) / (partial z)$ is positive if it increases as $x$ increases (with constant $y$).
