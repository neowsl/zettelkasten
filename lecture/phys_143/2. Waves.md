### Complex wave equation

Both position and time behaviour of a wave are sinusoidal. This suggests the function $D(x, t)$ is separable into two parts:

$$ D(x, t) = D_1(x) D_2(t) $$

If both parts repeat every $lambda$ or $T$, respectively,

$$ D(x, t) prop e^(i 2 pi x \/ lambda) e^(i 2 pi t \/ T) $$

We can simplify the notation by substituting:
- $w$ (angular frequency) = $2pi \/ T$
- $k$ (wavenumber) = $2pi \/ lambda$

Thus giving us:

$$ D(x, t) prop e^(i(k x + omega t)) $$

Now, we just have to add our amplitude $A$ (which can be complex!). Also, we need to be wary of the signs we use for $omega t$. Specifically,

$$ D(x, t) = A e^(i(k x plus.minus omega t)) $$

Suppose we use a plus sign. Focusing on a specific peak, as $t$ increases, $x$ must decrease to match the peak. Therefore, a plus sign implies the wave moves to the *left*.

Now suppose we use a minus sign. Focusing on a specific peak, as $t$ increases, $x$ must also increase to match the peak. Therefore, a minus sign implies the wave moves to the *right*.

### Double slit

Interference pattern of double slit is simply the combination of two single slits.

Note: The $Delta phi'$ in our complex wave equation is not a physical quantity; it is simply the difference in phase (expressed as an angle) between two sources.

$$ Delta phi' = (2 pi d) / (lambda L) y $$

$$ I prop A^2 prop |D(x, t)|^2 prop D(x, t) D^*(x, t) $$

$$ I prop {[2a cos((Delta phi') / 2)] e^(i(k x - omega t))} {[2a^* cos((Delta phi') / 2)] e^(-i(k x - omega t))} $$
$$ prop 4|a|^2 cos^2((Delta phi') / 2) $$
$$ prop 4I_1 cos^2((pi d) / (lambda L) y) $$

Note: The time dependence in the final term fell out, which shows us the interference pattern!

## 2026-05-01

Consider two waves with different frequencies (but same amplitudes):

$$ a_1 e^(i(k_1 x - omega_1 t)) + a_2 e^(i(k_2 x - omega_2 t)) $$

- $|a_1| = |a_2|$
- $omega_1 != omega_2, k_1 != k_2$
- To make things easier, let's pick $t_0$ to be when they are in sync: $a_1 = a_2$
- And we can pick $x = 0$, where we observe the wave (removes the $k$ terms)

Let's define:
- $omega_"avg" = (omega_1 + omega_2) / 2$
- $Delta omega = omega_1 - omega_2$

Then,

$$ D(x, t) = a e^(-i(omega_"avg" + (Delta omega) / 2)) + a e^(-i(omega_"avg" - (Delta omega) / 2)) $$
$$ D(x, t) = a [e^(-i (Delta omega) / 2 t) + e^(i (Delta omega) / 2 t)] e^(-i omega_"avg" t) $$

Using $(e^(i theta) + e^(-i theta)) / 2 = cos(theta)$,

$$ D(x, t) = [2a cos((Delta omega) / 2 t)] e^(-i omega_"avg" t) $$

Therefore, if these were "notes", we would hear the *average note*, but the amplitude fluctuates slowly.
