## 2026-05-11

Particles move like waves.

Real waves have both:
- a packet length
- a finite range of wavelengths (frequencies, k)

Particle waves have both. But remember that $lambda = h / p$. Therefore, since particle waves have a range of wavelengths, they can't have a single momentum.

### Heisenberg Uncertainty Principle

$$ Delta x Delta p >= planck.reduce / 2 $$

Two definitions of **uncertainty**:
- The particle has a specific $x, t, p$, but we don't know what it is. "Internal uncertainty" is abut the observer.
- The wave is fundamental. $x, t, p$ are **indeterminate**.

Bell's inequality rules out all internal variable models. Therefore, only the second definition is true.

### Overlapping packets

When photons move close together, they like to "bunch" together.

Each photon has a packet length, meaning each photon has a range of colours. Starlight photons bunch at very small lengths of packets, meaning starlight is innately *white*!

When we pass white light through a filter (e.g. a green filter), the filter makes a **partial measurement**. The photons passing through must collapse the probability of that range of colour.
