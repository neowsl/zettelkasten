## 2026-03-30

We can think of variables in equations as the *adjectives* in a sentence.

$T$: Period (s)
$A$: Amplitude (m)
$phi$: Initial position

Observations:
- Motion repeats
- Period does not depend on amplitude.
- More mass = longer period
- Stiffer spring = shorter period

## 2026-04-06

Real form: $A cos(i omega t)$
Complex form: $A e^(i omega t)$

Phase change is not part of the exponent; instead, it is part of the amplitude, which can be complex.

## 2026-04-08

For a swinging pendulum at small $theta$:
$$ F = -m g sin(theta) approx -m g theta $$

The bob travels a distance of $L theta$.

Therefore, we solve
$$ -m g theta(t) = m d^2 / (d t^2) L theta(t) $$

Let $theta(t) prop e^(alpha t)$:
$-g theta(t) = d^2 / (d t^2) L theta(t)$
$-g e^(alpha t) = L d^2 / (d t^2) e^(alpha t)$
$-g e^(alpha t) = L d / (d t) alpha e^(alpha t)$
$-g e^(alpha t) = L alpha^2 e^(alpha t)$
$-g = L alpha^2$
$alpha^2 = -g / L$
$alpha = i sqrt(g / L)$

This suggests that the pendulum oscillates with $omega = sqrt(g / L)$!

---

What about damped oscillations?

$$ -k x - b v = m a $$

$-k x(t) - b d / (d t) x(t) = m d^2 / (d t^2) x(t)$
$x(t) prop e^(alpha t)$
$-k e^(alpha t) - b alpha e^(alpha t) = m alpha^2 e^(alpha t)$
$-k - b alpha = m alpha^2$
$m alpha^2 + b alpha + k = 0$
$alpha = (-b plus.minus sqrt(b^2 - 4 m k)) / (2 m)$
