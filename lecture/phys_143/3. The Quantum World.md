## 2026-04-24

**Huygen's Principle**: The wavefront of a wave is composed of small "points" that themselves produce waves. We see a "plane" due to the sheer amount of points lined up.

![[Pasted image 20260424094450.png]]

### Single slit

Can imagine each point on the wall as a definite (bounded) integral, summing up contribution from each point wave from the wavefront.

$$ a / 2 sin theta = p lambda / 2 $$

Where $p = 1, 2, 3, ...$

Width of centre is twice as wide, width between dark spots remains constant.

Small angle approximation:

$$ y_p = (p lambda L) / d $$

Where $y_p$ is the position of the ${} p$-th minimum.

Note: For there to be a solution to the first equation, $sin(theta) <= 1$. Therefore, we have a limiting case: $(p lambda) / a <= 1$ in order for minima to appear.

### Double slit

Interference pattern of double slit is simply the combination of two single slits.

Note: The $Delta phi'$ in our complex wave equation is not a physical quantity; it is simply the difference in phase (expressed as an angle) between two sources.

$$ Delta phi' = (2 pi d) / (lambda L) y $$

$$ I prop A^2 prop |D(x, t)|^2 prop D(x, t) D^*(x, t) $$

$$ I prop {[2a cos((Delta phi') / 2)] e^(i(k x - omega t))} {[2a^* cos((Delta phi') / 2)] e^(-i(k x - omega t))} $$
$$ prop 4|a|^2 cos^2((Delta phi') / 2) $$
$$ prop 4I_1 cos^2((pi d) / (lambda L) y) $$

Note: The time dependence in the final term fell out, which shows us the interference pattern!

$$ y_m = (m lambda L) / d $$

## 2026-04-27

In the double slit experiment, if we turn down the laser until we only send one photon at a time, the photons still end up on the fringes! This suggests that each photon takes both paths simultaneously...

$$ E = h f = (h c) / lambda $$

> **Wave particle duality**
> Particles **move** like waves and **hit** like particles.

## 2026-04-29

Waves on a wall, what we see is intensity:

$$ I prop |A|^2 $$

$I$ has units of (energy / (area time)).

---

In a single photon movie, for each photon, $E = h f$. We see a probability density:

$$ P(x) prop |D(x)|^2 $$

$P(x)$ also has units of (energy / (area time)).1

If we calculate it out,

$$ P(Delta phi) = 4|a|^2 cos((Delta phi) / 2) $$

Recall: Particles move like waves and hit like particles.
- Moving like waves: Use $D(x, t)$
- Hitting like particles: Use $|D(x, t)|^2$
